# Competitor field notes

Research date: 30 October 2026. Company claims are labelled as such. These are desk-research findings, not interviews, a market survey or an audit of every app. Download counts are Play Store bands, not active users. Commercial information and prices can change.

## 1. e-NAM and Agmarknet — government baseline

**Who and channel:** Government market information and trading system. e-NAM has a website and mobile app; Agmarknet publishes reports via a portal. The [Agmarknet 2.0 Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=in.gov.agmarknet) describes a data-entry app for designated market officials, so do not assume all its installs are farmers.

**Product:** [Government statement, 16 December 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2204750&lang=2&reg=48) says e-NAM provides current market prices, nearby e-NAM markets and directions; it also supports electronic bidding and payments. The same statement says 4,367 mandis were linked to Agmarknet and 1,522 to e-NAM at that date. These are coverage figures, not user counts. e-NAM's [user guide](https://enam.gov.in/web/docs/eNAMMobileApp.pdf) illustrates nearby markets and price screens.

**Data:** Official market price and arrival reporting. Market reports are not guaranteed quotes for an individual farmer. Coverage and reporting dates differ.

**Money:** Government service; no evidence in these sources of a farmer subscription. Public funding is not a private startup revenue model.

**Feedback:** We have not yet sampled enough recent reviews to establish recurring praise or complaints. Test the actual farmer experience rather than guessing.

**Implication:** Displaying nearby mandi rates and directions is not a new idea. To justify our solution, demonstrate whether an after-cost comparison improves an actual user's decision.

## 2. Mai Kisaan — direct comparison in Maharashtra

**Who and channel:** The [company site](https://www.maikisaan.com/) describes a B2B marketplace for farmers, FPOs, traders and buyers, with website and mobile apps in English, Hindi and Marathi.

**Product:** It advertises buyer/seller enquiries, market prices and business contacts. Its [Nashik onion page](https://www.maikisaan.com/mandi-bhav/onion/maharashtra/nashik/) lists market, variety, grade, price and date; the contents change over time.

**Data:** The site says it sources mandi figures from Agmarknet and updates daily. It also asks sellers for commodity, quantity and expected price in enquiries. Its exact retention, algorithm and third-party data use require examining its current privacy policy and terms; do not assume it sells data.

**Money:** Its FAQ says posting and browsing are free while subscription unlocks contact details, full mandi prices and the business directory. No verified number of paying subscribers, revenue or profit is available from the inspected material.

**Reach and feedback:** The [Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.maikisaan.android) displayed 10K+ downloads at inspection; this is not an active-user figure. A balanced review sample is not established. Marketing claims of verified buyers have not been independently assessed.

**Implication:** Marathi, no-install website access, prices, buyer discovery and subscriptions already exist. Compare a specific task, not feature checkboxes.

## 3. ACROP — reports and alerts

**Who and channel:** [ACROP](https://acrop.app/) describes web and mobile tools for farmers, traders and buyers, and identifies Sangli, Maharashtra as its base.

**Product:** Market reports, price and arrival history, crop information and an AI question feature; some sensor and analytics ideas are labelled in development. The company explicitly states that Agmarknet reports are not guaranteed bids.

**Data:** Reported market information with dates and units; product-specific collection and algorithms were not audited. It warns that coverage is not equivalent to the number of markets reporting today. Its [privacy policy effective March 2026](https://acrop.app/policies/privacy-policy) describes collecting AI queries, images and usage information for its AI features; it says its website's market-price feature uses approximate IP-based state detection and government data. These are policy claims, not a technical audit.

**Money:** Its site advertises WhatsApp reports starting at ₹150/month; a listed price does not demonstrate customers, revenue or profitability.

**Reach and feedback:** The site lists catalogue coverage, not active users. Independent praise/complaint samples remain unknown.

**Implication:** Accessible reports, freshness, multilingual support and paid updates are already in the market. Our cost comparison must be evaluated against its actual workflow.

## 4. Agrowave — prices plus logistics

**Who and channel:** [NITI Aayog's 2023 agri-startup compendium](https://niti.gov.in/sites/default/files/2023-03/Compendium-of-75-Agri-Entrepreneur-and-Innovators.pdf) describes farmer, trader and transporter apps and farm-to-market logistics.

**Product:** Historical description includes mandi rates, transport prices, market choice and farm-gate sales. Current availability and geographic coverage have not been confirmed.

**Data and money:** Its supply-chain model depends on farmer, trader and transport information; exact current sources, charges, revenue and profitability are not established by the document. Aggregating farmer and logistics demand is described in the source, but no current algorithmic-performance claims can be inferred.

**Reach and feedback:** No current, independently confirmed active-user count or balanced review sample.

**Implication:** It is especially important not to claim that combining prices and transport is wholly unprecedented.

## 5. DeHaat — broader farm-to-market business

**Who and channel:** [Company business model](https://agrevolution.in/business-model) describes a network of physical centres that supports inputs, advisory and sales. Its [farmer app listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=app.intspvt.com.farmer) advertises weather, crop guidance, community and mandi prices.

**Data:** The [2022 Kisan app privacy policy](https://policies.agrevolution.in/kisan-app/privacy-policy/) describes personal and device information and credit-assessment use; its [FAQ](https://policies.agrevolution.in/kisan-app/faq/) describes basic farm and location data. These sources are dated and not fully consistent. Current runtime collection and source of individual mandi prices are unverified.

**Money:** [FY25 filing coverage](https://inc42.com/buzz/dehaat-posts-inr-369-cr-profit-in-fy25-on-back-of-non-cash-gains/) reports revenue from inputs/services and agricultural outputs, and explains a material non-cash gain behind the reported net profit. This is an organisation-wide historical result, not profit from an app feature.

**Reach:** The Play listing showed 100,000+ installs and approximately 1.28K reviews when inspected; installations and reviews are not active user figures. Company-wide farmer reach is a different metric.

**Feedback:** Two visible 2020 reviews praise services/advisory and ask for better app usability. A third-party aggregator mentions login and service issues, but the sample and methodology are unclear. No claim about the most common complaint is justified.

## 6. Kisan Network — physical market linkage

**Who and channel:** [Company website](https://kisannetwork.com/) describes a farm-to-business supply chain with sourcing, sorting, grading, packaging and delivery. Current app status is not established.

**Data:** The company says its rural management portal digitally captures supply-chain steps. It describes using information operationally for sourcing, pricing and quality; its actual data-sharing terms need separate review.

**Money:** A business-to-business produce supply operation is described; exact fees, margins and profitability are not publicly established by this review.

**Reach:** Its page states 80,000+ farmers and 10,000+ villages, with no clear reporting date; company-reported cumulative reach should not be presented as current active app users.

**Feedback:** Its site shows customer testimonials, which are selected marketing material, not an independent representative review sample.

## 7. BigHaat — indirect, agriculture input comparison

**Channel and offering:** [Play Store listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.BigHaat) advertises a farm-input marketplace, expert guidance, crop tools, community and delivery. This is an indirect competitor, not evidence that its core offering is mandi decision support.

**Business and data:** Input sales are advertised; fees, margins and full data uses need primary-source verification. The Play listing's developer-reported data safety section is not an audit.

**Scale and feedback:** The listing showed 1M+ downloads and a 4.3-star rating when inspected. Three visible reviews dated June–August 2026 complain about delivery, support and product search. Those are individual accounts, not prevalence estimates and not mandi-feature reviews.

## What is still missing

No competitor's complete profitability, exact market share or active-user count has been established here. We have not installed and systematically tested each app, taken a representative review sample, or spoken with farmers/FPOs. The next field test should observe how a farmer currently chooses a mandi, obtains a price, estimates transport and reacts to our comparison; do not lead them toward the product. Ask an FPO what outcome would justify paying, and what it currently spends on the problem.
