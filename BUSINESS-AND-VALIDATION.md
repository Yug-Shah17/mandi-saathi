# Business and validation plan

## Customer and user are not necessarily the same person

The intended user is a farmer deciding where to sell a particular crop. A potential paying customer could be an FPO, cooperative or agricultural advisory organisation that supports many farmers. The idea is to offer its staff a simple, transparent decision tool while keeping access affordable for farmers. Neither willingness to pay nor the farmer benefit has been validated.

## The problem with the business hypothesis

Prices are available from government and commercial platforms. A subscription is only defensible if the buyer gets a measurable improvement that alternatives do not provide cheaply enough. Questions to test:

1. Does the organisation already help members compare markets and transport? How much time does that consume?
2. What information is missing at the moment a farmer makes the decision? Are actual bids or transport availability more important than reported modal prices?
3. Can staff regularly obtain dependable, crop-specific prices and costs? Who keeps the data current?
4. Would the organisation pay for the tool, at what price, and who approves the decision? Would a pilot be needed first?
5. How many members use it frequently enough to justify onboarding, support and language maintenance?

## Unit economics: a worksheet, not a forecast

Annual contribution per organisation = subscription revenue − variable data, support, onboarding and distribution costs. To estimate business profit, subtract salaries, product maintenance, insurance and other fixed costs across all customers. The figures are unknown; do not use unverified price or customer projections as evidence.

Illustration only: if one organisation paid ₹12,000/year and directly attributable annual support, data and onboarding cost ₹4,000, it would contribute ₹8,000 before fixed expenses and taxes. If fixed annual expenses were ₹4 lakh, 50 such customers would cover those assumed expenses (₹4,00,000 / ₹8,000); this is a simplified break-even calculation, not a forecast, quote or evidence that anyone will pay.

## Competing hypotheses

- **FPO subscription:** possible recurring revenue, but organisations may not have a budget or may already use free services.
- **Buyer-sponsored intelligence:** buyers may pay for aggregated market information, but incentives can conflict with farmers' interests and personal data must not be sold without appropriate consent and clear purpose.
- **Transaction/logistics commission:** revenue tied to completed services; requires real partners, reliable operations and transparent fees. The current app does not provide this service.
- **Direct farmer subscription:** simple in theory, but adoption, ability to pay and trust are unknown. A published competitor price does not prove demand.

Use the FPO model as the first hypothesis, not as a proven business model.

## Farmer interview guide

Ask about a recent real sale before presenting the concept. Do not record identifiable or sensitive information unless needed and consented to.

1. What crop and quantity did you sell? Which market did you choose and why?
2. Where did you get the price? How old was it, and did the actual bid match?
3. What transport, commissions and other costs did you face? Which were unknown beforehand?
4. What made you sell that day instead of waiting? Was storage available, and what could spoil?
5. Show the prototype with fictitious examples. Ask the person to explain the result and what they would check before travelling. Observe confusion without coaching.
6. What would you change? What could make the advice misleading? Would you trust it, and why?

## Practical success criteria for a pilot

- A farmer can explain that reported prices are historical information, not offers.
- A farmer can enter quantity and costs, identify the after-cost comparison and understand its limitations unaided.
- A farmer understands that the hold calculation uses their assumed future price and is not a forecast.
- The data source, units, crop variety and reporting date can be verified for any displayed record.
- An FPO representative can describe the benefit and either articulate a credible budget or explain why the service has no value to them.

These are proposed checks; none has been completed with farmers or an FPO yet.
