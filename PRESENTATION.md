# Workshop presentation and demo guide

## The problem
Farmers deciding where to sell need more than a high displayed price: transport and other charges can change the amount they take home. Government market reports are useful information, but a reported price is not a guaranteed offer.

## Our proposed solution
Mandi Saathi is a lightweight mobile-friendly web prototype that compares two markets using entered quantity, price, transport and other costs. It also lets users explore a sell-later scenario using their own assumed price, waiting costs and spoilage. This scenario is arithmetic, not a forecast or advice to wait. It works without an account or installation. English and Marathi interface text are available. The examples are fictional, not current mandi prices. The app does not connect automatically to a price feed.

## Live demo
1. Open the app on a phone or resize the browser.
2. Explain the visible fictional-data notice. Use five quintals at ₹2,000, transport ₹400 versus ₹2,150, transport ₹1,200.
3. Calculate. Market A estimates ₹9,600 and Market B ₹9,550. Despite the higher quoted price at B, A is ₹50 higher after the entered costs.
4. Change the quantity or transport and calculate again. Explain that actual price, quality, fees and availability need checking before travel.
5. Switch the interface to Marathi. Show the official source links for the user to check prices.
6. In the sell-later scenario, the defaults yield ₹10,000 now versus ₹9,950 later after assumed loss and waiting costs, with a break-even later price of ₹2,210.53/qtl. Explain that the future price is entered by the user, not predicted.

## Competition and business
e-NAM and Agmarknet already show price information and market discovery; Agrowave has described transport information. Our hypothesis is a simple, transparent net-proceeds workflow, not a claim that no competitor does this. An FPO subscription is a possible business model, not validated revenue. Research details and sources are in RESEARCH.md.

## Honest limits and next steps
No farmer interviews, user testing, live feed, prediction, transaction service or proof of commercial demand yet. Next: select a Maharashtra district and crop, verify the dataset and access, speak to farmers and FPOs, test usefulness and language, and explore historical trends only if data supports them. A prototype does not establish income gains.

## Likely questions
- Why not e-NAM? It already offers useful market information. This prototype explores an additional cost comparison, whose incremental benefit still needs testing.
- How do you make money? A potential FPO or cooperative service is a hypothesis. We need interviews, pricing and cost estimates before asserting profitability.
- Is this real-time? No. The demo uses fictional values; a person can enter independently verified figures. The source links lead to official systems.
- Is it an AI model? No. The calculator is deterministic arithmetic. No forecast or AI capability is claimed.
- What about when to sell? A what-if calculator shows how waiting costs and losses affect the break-even price; it does not predict or recommend a selling date. A data-backed recommendation would require suitable historical data, evaluation and farmer validation.
